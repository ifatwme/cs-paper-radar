How the radar works

Coverage

Every day the pipeline asks the arXiv API for all papers first submitted in these categories (primary or cross-listed): {{ settings.arxiv_categories|join(', ') }}. The last {{ settings.update_lookback_days }} days are re-fetched on every run, because arXiv publishes a day's submissions only after its evening announcement and updates (new versions, acceptance notes) arrive later.

The frontier score

arXiv does not publish citations, downloads or votes, so new papers are ranked with signals that can be computed from the paper itself and from the rest of the corpus. Every signal is in [0, 1]; the score is their weighted sum. Each paper page shows the value and the evidence for every signal.

SignalWeightWhat it measures
Venue{{ (weights.venue*100)|round|int }}%The comment or journal reference says the paper was accepted at, or appears in, a recognised venue (NeurIPS, CCS, USENIX Security, SIGCOMM, OSDI, ICSE, STOC, ...). Workshops count less; "under review" counts little.
Code/data{{ (weights.artifacts*100)|round|int }}%Code, data, models or a project page are released (GitHub, Hugging Face, Zenodo links, or an explicit statement). Promised releases count half.
Momentum{{ (weights.momentum*100)|round|int }}%How many papers of the same week sit close to this one in embedding space, compared with what the previous {{ settings.momentum_baseline_weeks }} weeks predict. A high value means its research direction is accelerating. Percentile within the week.
Novelty{{ (weights.novelty*100)|round|int }}%Distance to the closest paper published in the previous {{ settings.novelty_lookback_days }} days. Percentile within the week.
Breadth{{ (weights.breadth*100)|round|int }}%Cross-listed in several categories, especially across different research areas.
Substance{{ (weights.substance*100)|round|int }}%A full paper (8+ pages, figures, journal version) rather than a short note.
Team{{ (weights.team*100)|round|int }}%Collaboration size, log-scaled and capped at 10 authors. Deliberately weak.

Momentum and novelty pull in opposite directions on purpose: one rewards joining a fast-moving direction, the other rewards leaving the crowd. The weekly top {{ settings.top_n }} takes at most {{ settings.top_max_per_category }} papers per primary category and skips near-duplicates, so one busy field cannot fill the list. Weights are configurable (SCORE_WEIGHTS), and citation counts or community votes can be added as further signals when more sources are connected.

Topics

A topic query is matched two ways: BM25 keyword search over titles and abstracts (all words must appear), and semantic search with sentence embeddings ({{ settings.embedding_model }}, run locally). Results are fused by reciprocal rank. For the matching papers the radar shows:

  • What's new — the most recent matching papers.
  • Frontier — the highest frontier scores among clearly relevant papers in the window.
  • Emerging terms — phrases whose share of the topic's papers rose most compared with the preceding period (log-odds z-score).
  • Research directions — clusters of the topic's papers (spherical k-means on embeddings), labelled by their most distinctive phrases.
  • Trend — the topic's share of all CS/CE papers per week.

API

Everything on the site is available as JSON — see the interactive API docs. For example /api/weekly?n=20, /api/daily?date=YYYY-MM-DD&cat=cs.CR, /api/topic?q=network%20security.

Limits

  • The score estimates potential, not quality. It cannot read the paper; a brilliant single-author preprint with no code will rank lower than it deserves.
  • Venue detection relies on what authors write in the arXiv comment field.
  • Days are UTC submission days, not arXiv announcement days.
